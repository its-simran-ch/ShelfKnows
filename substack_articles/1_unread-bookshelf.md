# The Psychology of the Unread Bookshelf: Why We Buy Books Faster Than We Actually Read Them

> **Subtitle / Preview:** *When I started getting into reading, I bought around fifteen books with so much excitement. But soon, half of them were just sitting in my bookshelf untouched. Here are my thoughts on why we do this, and why we shouldn't feel that much guilty about it...*

---

When I first started building a reading habit, I got a little (actually, *quiteee*) excited.

I visited bookstores, searched online, added books to my wishlist, and wanted to purchase every single book that made me think deeply, the ones whose covers looked aesthetic, and also the ones whose hook on the back made me think: **This book is going to change my life and I'll turn into a completely different person after reading it.**

I scrolled through online recommendations, saving every post and video that mentioned something like: *'7 books you should read if you're an overthinker'*, *'5 books that will change your life completely'*, *'10 books you should read in your 20s'*, etc.

I ended up buying around fifteen books in just a few weeks. *Fifteennnnnnn?* (Average behavior of someone trying to build a reading habit!). I can't even explain the feeling of holding them in my hands - **I had already started feeling productive, even before reading a single one of them.**

In my head, I had this whole aesthetic plan: I was going to read every single one of them, one after another with a warm cup of chai, even though **I had already assumed almost every second book would be my 'first read'.**

Fast forward a couple of months, and reality looked very different from my imagination (as always). I had finished maybe three or four - that too with many breaks in between because life happened, obviously. The rest were just sitting in my bookshelf, **looking like I had just built my own little library of unread books.**

And honestly? I started feeling this weird sense of guilt every time I looked at those unread books:

*Why did I spend money on these if I'm not even reading them? Did I buy them just to make my bookshelf look aesthetic? Or simply because their covers were so catchy?*

And suddenly there comes a question in my mind...


## Is There Something Wrong With Having Unread Books?

Not necessarily!

While trying to figure this out, I found something really comforting: **buying books we never get around to reading is actually a super common habit.**

In fact, in Japan, there is a dedicated word for it: **Tsundoku (積読)** - the habit of buying books and just collecting them without reading them.

Suddenly, my guilt started melting away. I was like: *Wait whattt, you mean there is an actual term for this habit of mine? Almost every reader experiences this exact same thing?*  It was a complete *'us bro us'* moment!

See, **the problem isn't really having unread books. The problem is believing that owning a book and actually experiencing what it contains are the exact same thing.**


> A bookshelf should inspire curiosity, not become a monument to our unfinished ambitions.

When I started looking deeper into the psychology behind it, I discovered that having unread books isn't always a sign of laziness. Sometimes, it actually reveals something really interesting about our minds - starting with pure dopamine.


## The Dopamine of Buying Books Feels Way More Satisfying Than Actually Reading Them

There is something insanely satisfying about discovering a book online, adding it to your cart, hitting *'Buy Now'*, and then checking the tracking status five times a day as if your life depends on it (I mean, the anticipation is so real!).

And then... the package finally arrives!

You unbox it, admire the cover from five different angles, sniff the pages, snap a quick aesthetic photo for your Instagram story (obviously with some chill lo-fi music in the background), and gently place it on your bookshelf.

In reality, we haven't even read the first page yet - but it already feels like *mission accomplished!*

Here is the psychology behind it:

Our brains are addicted to the thrill of novelty. *Clicking 'Order' gives you an instant, effortless hit of dopamine in literally three seconds.*

*Reading, on the other hand? That requires patience, deep focus, putting your phone away, and actual mental effort.*


> *Buying a book is a fun 2-minute dopamine rush, but reading a book is a 300-page relationship commitment. And sometimes, our tired brains just prefer the quick dopamine hit.*

Now, this doesn't mean every unread book on your bookshelf is a crime or a sign of laziness.

Life gets chaotic, busy schedules happen, or maybe a book simply didn't match the exact vibe you expected at that moment — and that's completely okay.

But more often than not? We just fell completely in love with the excitement of *starting* something new, rather than the quiet patience of actually following through.


## The Illusion of Productivity: When Buying Books Feels Like Actually Reading Them

Here is where things get even more interesting: **Buying books can trick our brains into feeling like we're already being productive, without doing any actual reading.**


> When we buy a book, we are somehow buying the hope of who we want to be, how we want to live, how we want to think, how we want to approach things...

When you pick up a book, you aren't just paying for paper and ink. You are imagining an upgraded, ideal version of yourself — *the person who wakes up early, sits peacefully with a warm cup of chai, reads about psychology and business, and never gets lost in endless phone scrolling.*

*Want to fix your overthinking? You order two psychology bestsellers. Want to get your life together? You buy three self-growth books with minimalist covers and catchy titles.*

And for a solid five minutes, you lean back feeling like: **Yes, I am finally fixing my entire life.** (Even though you haven't changed 1% yet!).

Owning a 300-page book doesn't magically make you smarter. Using cute pastel highlighters doesn't guarantee you *actually* understood the concept. And having a bookshelf packed with personal growth books doesn't mean you've suddenly transformed into a disciplined monk.

Curiously, our minds confuse simply owning a book with having actually absorbed its knowledge (bro, I really wish it worked like that so back then in school I would have had good chemistry with *Chemistry... anyway!*).

Collecting books becomes this super comfortable substitute for *actual* effort. It is honestly the sneakiest form of procrastination in existence—because to the outside world, you look like a focused, intellectual human working on self-growth.


> After all, who would ever criticize someone for buying books, right?


## Why the Guilt Isn't Really Needed?

Earlier, I used to feel guilty whenever I looked at the unread books on my bookshelf. But there is a much kinder way to look at it - and discovering this changed my perspective a little bit.


### **The Idea of the 'Antilibrary'**

I came across a concept coined by author *Nassim Nicholas Taleb* called the **'Antilibrary.'** He based it on the Italian writer *Umberto Eco*, who owned a massive home library with thousands of books.

When visitors asked if he had read *all* of them, Eco pointed out something really interesting: **the books you haven't read are actually far more valuable than the ones you've finished.**

*A shelf holding only books you've read just shows off what you already know. But a shelf with unread books is a humble reminder of your curiosity - it constantly inspires you to learn more.*


> The true value of a bookshelf lies not in what you have *consumed*, but in what you have yet to *discover*.

**What a massive perspective shift this was for me!**


### So if your bookshelf currently looks like a tiny library of untouched books...

Please don't feel guilty about your unread books. You didn't waste your money, and reading is supposed to bring you *peace*, not guilt. You might not need that story today or next week. But on some random quiet evening with a warm cup of chai, you'll pick up that exact book and smile thinking: *Ah, I’m finally reading this book.*

**Till then, enjoy your picked up first read... ☕**
