# The 10 PM Switch: What Replacing Late-Night Reels with a Book Did to My Brain

> **Subtitle / Preview:** *I used to spend every night scrolling Instagram reels till 11 P.M. or i would say until my eyes burned, waking up exhausted and full of guilt. So I tried an experiment: reading a book in bed instead of staring at my phone. Here are my experience on how that simple switch fixed my attention span...*

---


### Until a few months ago, my bedtime routine was a complete disaster.

Every night around 10:00 PM, I would get into bed, open Instagram, and endlessly scroll through reels (I guess approximately till 11:00 PM).

I would keep scrolling and scrolling until my eyes literally started burning and felt too heavy to stay open. And only then would I finally put the phone aside and fall asleep.


### The result?

I would wake up the next morning feeling exhausted, with a heavy head and this annoying sense of guilt:

*Why do I waste hours endlessly scrolling reels every single night instead of just sleeping?*

In my defense, my brain always gave me this easy excuse:

*Simran, you spent the entire day studying at university, attended lectures, and then did self-study at home. You worked so hard all day - scrolling reels and turning your brain off is your well-deserved chill time!*


### Except, it was not 'chill' at all.

I mean **my sleep was restless, I was always feeling guilty about wasting time, and honestly, endless scrolling had completely ruined my reading attention span too...**


## And when I say ruined, I mean it

Whenever I tried to read a book, an online article, or even learn something from ChatGPT, I found myself constantly rushing and skipping words.

The second I spotted a sentence that looked even slightly familiar, my impatient brain would go: *Oh yeah, yeah, I already know this,* and rush ahead.

But skipping through text always left me with this frustrating guilt:

*Why am I in such a hurry? Why can't I just slow down, be patient, and read word-by-word? What if I just missed something really important?*

One day when my mother asked me about my day at university, I shared all these above mentioned chaotic thoughts with her and then she said:


> *What you feed your mind right before sleeping directly controls your mood, your focus, and your energy for the entire next day.*

And suddenly that thought started hitting hard on my mind, and I was like: *Okay, I need to change that late-night Instagram scrolling habit right away, because I can't afford any sort of guilt anymore.*


### So, I decided to try a simple change:

*I replaced my phone (or I would say my Instagram scrolling habit) with reading a physical book before bed.* (i tried avoiding reading books online back then because somehow after reading 5 or 6 pages on my phone, i will end up scrolling random reels as a part of a little break)

At first, it felt tough. I mean *after studying all day (not like every second but I hope you get it), picking up another book felt harder than picking up my phone and scrolling reels, right?*

To be honest, reading wasn't completely new to me - I used to read earlier, but honestly, over the past two years, because of college life, hectic classes, and studies i couldn't able to read any book apart from my textbooks.

So this time, when I decided to bring it back, I didn't put any strict pressure on myself. I don't follow a fixed target of pages - it completely depends on my time and mood.

Some nights when I'm exhausted, I read just two or three pages to maintain the consistency. Other nights when I have the time, a little bit of energy and genuinely feel like sitting down to read, I easily end up reading fifteen or twenty pages.

What truly matters isn't the number of pages; it's that **picking up a physical book before bed has quietly become my favorite, screen-free peace.**


### And the difference it made in my everyday routine still surprises me.

That heavy morning exhaustion and the annoying guilt of *Why did I scroll Instagram reels so late again?* slowly started disappearing.

*Although I heard that there are people who literally scroll Instagram reels till 1 a.m. or even till 2 a.m., so if I compare myself with them then I guess 11 p.m. is not so late right? But justttt because, justtt because I do not compare myself with others, so I have to say: YES, SCROLLING INSTAGRAM REELS TILL 11 p.m. IS VERYYY LATE MITTARRR...*

For the first time in months, I was waking up with a fresh, clear head.

Even better, my brain slowly stopped rushing for quick dopamine — I could finally sit with a book without rushing or skipping through sentences.


### It made me stop and wonder:

*Why did something so simple feel like a complete brain reset? What actually happens inside our brain waves and subconscious mind when we read in bed in the dark instead of staring at a glowing screen?*

Being the curious person I am, I went searching for the science behind it on the internet — and honestly, what I found was so interesting. Let me explain what I found in my words:


## The Brain Wave Shift: The Neuroscience Behind It


### From Daily Chaos to Instant Peace

Throughout your busy day, your brain runs on fast brain waves called **Beta waves**. Beta waves are responsible for overthinking, planning, and mental stress.

When you try to sleep right after doomscrolling, your brain stays stuck in this fast, stressed-out mode.

But the moment you sit under a soft, warm yellow light and begin reading book pages, your brain slowly relaxes into **Alpha and Theta waves**.

*Your heart rate slows down, your muscles relax, and your body switches from alert mode to deep, restful sleep.*


## The 'Strict Logic Voice' Shuts Off: The Sleepy Zone

*Have you ever noticed how stories feel so much more real and magical when you read them while feeling sleepy?*

There is a really interesting reason for this called the **hypnagogic state —** it basically just means when you enter a dreamy middle state between being awake and falling asleep.

If you notice while reading a book during the day, if there is any thrilling or unexpected plot twist...our brain has this strict, practical voice that constantly reminds us: *Come on, this is just a made-up story on paper, not real life.*

This is your **prefrontal cortex —** the front part of your brain, the bossy manager who handles logic, facts, and reality checking.

As you get sleepy, your prefrontal cortex slowly goes to sleep before the rest of your mind. Means that practical voice is the very first thing to shut off and go to sleep!

Without that practical voice questioning everything, the wall between the book and your imagination slowly started fading, and then you slowly started entering in your own world full of imagination.

Suddenly, you don't just read about a rainy street in a story — you can almost hear the raindrops, feel the cold breeze, and hear the character's voice. The story feels 100% real, like you are actually living inside it.


> In that half-asleep midnight hour, reading isn't just an activity. It's a waking dream where the author whispers the story and your mind paints the reality.


## The Golden 30 Minutes: How Stories Turn Into Dreams?


> The last 30 minutes before sleep are like the 'ingredients' you give your subconscious mind to cook your dreams with.


### Now think about what happens when you spend those 30 minutes doomscrolling reels.

*In just twenty minutes, your brain consumes fifty completely random things — a meme, loud music, someone showing their aesthetic manali trip, company's layoff news, and people arguing in the comment section.*

Your mind goes to sleep with forty chaotic tabs open! It spends the entire night stressed out, sorting through all that random noise.


### A physical book does the exact opposite.

*When you feed your mind with a quiet, thoughtful story, your subconscious mind takes the peaceful thoughts and images from those pages and weaves them into your dreams.* It is almost like your brain calmly organizes your thoughts while you rest.

That is why waking up after reading a good book feels so completely different. You don't wake up feeling tired from processing random internet chaos — you wake up feeling refreshed and clear-headed.


## The Ultimate Escape from the Screen Hangover (my most favourite one because it gives major relief)


### So, the question is, why does that glowing screen make our mornings so heavy?

When you stare at your phone in a dark room, that harsh blue light shoots straight into your eyes. It tricks your brain into thinking it's still daytime.

It completely stops releasing **melatonin** (your body's natural sleep hormone) and keeps your mind in stressed-out awake mode.

A real paper book under a warm yellow lamp does the exact opposite: *it sends a clear signal to your body that night is here, and it is finally time to relax and sleep.*

But honestly? The biggest game-changer isn't just the light — it's the **guilt versus peace**.


### Okay, tell me one thing... when you fall asleep right after scrolling reels, what is the very last thought in your head?

In mine, it was a pure guilt.

My brain is silently beating itself up: *Why did I waste so much time on my phone again? Why do I do this every single night? Why i am not changing this habit of mine?*

That guilt stays with my subconscious all night. That hidden guilt keeps my mind stressed and anxious, which is why my sleep ends up so restless.


### Now if i compare this with closing a book after reading even 2 or 3 pages before sleeping. Things are different.

There is zero guilt. Instead, i feel calm, satisfied, and secretly proud of myself.

My sleep quality improved and I started waking up feeling refreshed and clear-headed because when your mind goes to sleep happy with itself, deep, healing sleep happens completely naturally.

Honestly, if there's one thing I’ve learned from this experiment, it's this:


> You don't need a crazy 10-step routine to fix your life. Sometimes, all it takes is simply swapping your late-night reels scrolling habit with reading a good book.
